Healthcare assistant (HCA) application tips

How to apply for healthcare assistant and care support worker jobs on NHS Jobs and TRAC: what shortlisters look for and how to show it without direct experience.

Healthcare assistant (HCA) and healthcare support worker posts are often advertised on NHS Jobs or TRAC, usually at Band 2 or Band 3. Many attract a large number of applications, so a clear, specific application helps you stand out.

1. Read the person specification

Typical essential criteria include good communication, kindness and respect for patients, teamwork, basic literacy and numeracy, and a willingness to complete training such as the Care Certificate. Check which criteria are assessed on the application form and answer those first.

2. Show care values with real examples

You do not always need paid care experience. Examples from retail, hospitality, volunteering or caring for a relative can show the same values: patience with a distressed customer, keeping someone’s dignity, following procedures, working shifts in a team. Use a short STAR structure for each (STAR method).

3. Mention relevant training and checks

  • Care Certificate, first aid, manual handling or basic life support if you have them.
  • Any previous enhanced DBS check (the employer will normally request a new one).
  • Flexibility for shifts, nights and weekends if the advert asks for it — only if it is true.

4. Get the employment history right

NHS forms ask for every job with dates and reasons for leaving, and they check gaps. Make dates match your CV and explain gaps briefly (explaining gaps).

5. Apply to several trusts, tailored

Posts close early when they get many applications, so apply promptly. Tailor the supporting information to each trust’s values and person specification rather than pasting the same text.
