How to fill in a job application form (UK)

A section-by-section walk through a typical UK job application form: personal details, employment history, education, questions, references and declarations.

Many UK employers ask for an application form instead of, or as well as, a CV. The sections are similar on most forms, whether it is NHS Jobs, TRAC, the Civil Service, a council or a company using Workday. Shortlisters compare candidates on the form, so a complete and accurate one matters.

Before you start

  • Read the advert, job description and person specification, and note the closing date.
  • Have your CV, dates of each job, qualifications with grades and dates, and two referees’ details to hand.
  • Write long answers in a document first and paste them in, so a timeout does not lose your work.

1. Personal details

Name, contact details and address. Use an email address you check often and a phone number you will answer. Check spelling — recruiters will use these to invite you to interview.

2. Employment history

List jobs most recent first, with employer, job title, start and end dates (usually month and year) and a short summary of duties. Many forms ask for the reason for leaving (see how to answer reason for leaving) and want gaps explained (see explaining gaps). Make the dates match your CV exactly.

3. Education and qualifications

Add each qualification with the subject, grade, place of study and date. Include professional registrations and memberships where the role asks for them.

4. Supporting information or questions

This is the part shortlisters read most closely. Answer against the person specification, one example per criterion (see the supporting statement guide and the STAR method). Keep within the word limit.

5. Eligibility, references and declarations

  • Right to work: answer honestly; you will usually be asked for evidence later (right to work questions).
  • References: usually your current or most recent employer (references guide).
  • Equality monitoring is normally optional and kept separate from shortlisting (equality forms).
  • Declarations are legal statements — read them yourself before ticking.

Final check

Read every page before you press submit: dates, job titles, attachments and spelling. Tools such as Snapply can fill the repeated sections (personal details, every job and qualification, your CV upload) from your CV, but you should always check the result and submit it yourself.
